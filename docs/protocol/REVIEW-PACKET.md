## 1. Summary (self-contained)

warOnSaaS's coordinator, wOS, leases small build units to contributors whose own Claude/ChatGPT subscriptions run the agents; two other contributors' agents (Astra on Codex, Fable on Claude) and a human review the work; the wOS GitHub App merges it. Amendment 02 adds an economy: **WOS**, a Solana token (devnet in V1), allocated by **Proof of Contribution**.

The design:
- **Budgets and receipts (D49).** Every commissioned task (build unit, agent or human review, audit, resolution, planning) carries a **budget in ACU fixed before work starts** (1 ACU = USD 1 of list-price API inference, never shown as money), from a versioned budget model, reviewed in consensus and compared with peers; budgets under one acceptance objective never exceed the objective's budget. An accepted task produces immutable ContributionReceipts carrying that budget and the collaborators' declared shares. Provider usage is **telemetry** (cap enforcement, budget calibration, signals). Proposals, bugs and security keep outcome weights.
- **Epochs (weekly).** Budget = 3,327 ppm of the remaining 995M-WOS reserve (≈ 4-year half-life), split 60/10/5/5/15/5 (execution, planning, human review — together the **task capacity** — outcomes, completion accrual, security reserve). Issuing a task **reserves** budget × the epoch's issuance rate (min(100 WOS/ACU decaying, capacity / queued demand)); a task that does not fit is not issued; acceptance pays the reservation, never scaled. One conserved equation: reserve + pools + security reserve + reserved budgets + issued = 995M.
- **Optimistic payouts.** CALCULATING (48 h: sampled audits 5%, canaries, bounded holds) → PROPOSED (every allocation public with an explanation and deterministic anomaly metrics; 48 h challenge window; silence accepts) → FINALIZED (entitlements, each consuming a named source balance once: released now, with a holdback — recommended 20% for 6 epochs — whose maturity is pinned when the tranche is created). Any epoch participant can dispute any set of allocations in one frozen bundle (per-item stake, floor 1 WOS, forfeited per rejected item); the dispute focuses a sealed payout-audit gate of 2 random outside-feature auditors on their own subscriptions; outcomes UPHELD/CLIPPED/REVOKED; one appeal, and money follows the effective final adjudication only; bounty 20% of what was RECOVERED. After a finding, a notice HOLDS protocol-held amounts (pending, holdback, unclaimed) up to the proven excess; the holds execute after the reply and appeal windows or an upheld appeal, and are released if overturned; then offsets, revocation, exclusion. Released tokens are never seized. Recovery is compensatory; punitive forfeiture awaits founder decision F17.
- **Bootstrap.** The founder may merge anything (public label) but his own work gets PROVISIONAL receipts that count only in devnet test epochs; when bootstrap ends each is published with a challenge window and finalizes on silence (D54; a challenge sends it to the review gate). Genesis credit for pre-protocol work is valued by accepted output only (retro size points × reference ACU × early rate), capped at 0.5% of supply, issued only at mainnet with 2-year vesting, approved by ≥ 2 non-founder humans.
- **Solana.** Token-2022 with metadata only; no freeze, no permanent delegate, no transfer hook. Devnet: claim-triggered push transfers — signed transaction persisted before broadcast, one active attempt per leaf, replacement only after a historical search, confirmation at finalized commitment, adapter generations fenced; epoch roots anchored by SPL Memo; no custom programs. Mainnet: an audited escrow releasing a few epochs at a time, mint revoked; migration drains in-flight transfers, then snapshots.
- **Governance.** Policy numbers are data, forward-only, previewed by the simulation. Founder mode until an activation threshold; then tiered supermajorities (60/70/75%, emergency > 50%) in BOTH locked-token (≥ 12 months) and 6-month contribution weight, denominators over distributed supply only, 10% cap per organization.
- **Organizations** can be beneficiaries; org-mates are related accounts for every independence rule.

- **V1-ACTIVE and DORMANT modules (D55).** V1 builds only what a solo founder on devnet/shadow with valueless tokens needs: accounting correctness, budgets, acceptance review (Astra + a human; Fable unavailable, D53), the optimistic challenge window, provisional finalization (D54), the audit trail, the shadow epoch pipeline, a simple bounded hold and the holdback, and "build next" assignment (D56). Dispute stakes and bounties, multi-allocation disputes and appeals, payout canaries, organization caps and splits, governance voting, advanced collusion detection, confiscation beyond a simple hold and the Genesis calibration population are **dormant**: designed and tested, refused until a forward-only activation after their trigger (PROTOCOL §13).

## 3d. What changed after the review-03 fix pass, and why: budget-based execution rewards (D49)

> Bundle 04 (the review-03 fix pass, before D49) **was reviewed** (review 04); the statement in the bundle-05 prompt that it was not is corrected above. Review 04's findings and their status are in §3f.

**The decision.** The founder replaced Amendment 02's A2 ("execution work is rewarded by token usage") with **budget-based rewards**: every commissioned task carries a reward budget in ACU fixed before work starts (decomposition / contract consensus: expected compute, difficulty, importance, shared-dependency value; an unjustified budget is a material finding for Astra and Fable; compared with peer units). Acceptance and merge earn the budget whatever tokens were used; collaborators split by declared shares (sum 10,000 bp). Usage becomes telemetry. Epochs cap issuance by **reservation at issuance**.

**Why.** (1) Usage on contributor-controlled machines cannot be verified: your review-02 H4 and review-03 M13; the simulation showed +29% to +30% expected gain at 0.1% detection per receipt even with a 50% holdback and recovery, +53% with contaminated baselines. (2) Usage pay penalised efficiency: a contributor who needed half the tokens was paid half. (3) The white paper already argued for paying accepted output. (4) Budget-based pay removes the usage-fraud surface by construction instead of patching it.
