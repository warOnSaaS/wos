# TOKENOMICS-SIMULATION (DRAFT, pending Astra review)

Reproducible simulation of the Proof of Contribution economics and abuse model. Everything below the marker is generated by `tools/tokenomics-sim` from a fixed seed and the protocol's own reward engine (`@waronsaas/contracts/protocol` `computeEpoch`, bigint, conserved funding equation). Nothing in it is data about real people: there are no contributors yet. All inputs are stated assumptions, chosen to explore the design, and the V1 policy values themselves are provisional (D33).

## How to run

```
source ~/.nvm/nvm.sh && nvm use 22            # Node >= 22.18 (runs .ts directly)
npm run sim:tokenomics                         # builds the contracts, prints the tables
node tools/tokenomics-sim/run.ts --write       # refreshes the tables in this file
node tools/tokenomics-sim/run.ts --check       # exits 1 if this file is stale (also run by the test suite)
node tools/tokenomics-sim/preview.ts <new-reward-policy.json>   # what-if: re-runs the scenarios under a candidate policy and diffs them (D33)
```

## What the simulation models

- **Epoch scenarios S1–S10** (520 weekly epochs = 10 years, checkpoints shown). Active contributors are split into 20 lognormal quantile cohorts (mean 20 ACU/week, sigma 0.8, so the median contributor produces about 14.5 ACU/week), 60% on Claude and 40% on Codex, with seeded noise. Contributor zero (the founder) is modelled separately at 30 ACU/week under the same rules. Planning weight is 10% of execution; human review is 1 ACU-equivalent per merged unit (12 ACU per unit); proposals are 10 ACU-equivalents per 50 units. Feature pools accrue per 12-epoch feature and pay at completion; one application pool pays at year 5; a medium security payout every 13 epochs. S7 models the oracle following a 40%/year inference price decline; S8 a 50% Codex price cut in oracle v2 at epoch 53; S9/S10 contributor zero alone with and without the rate ceiling.
- **A. Waste behaviours** (Astra-01 item 5): what cap saturation, context inflation, expensive-model choice, padded repairs, task splitting and a friendly reviewer earn relative to an honest median contributor, with the V1 caps, run-log haircut, sampled audits, disputes prompted by the anomaly ranking, and the 13-epoch pattern lookback.
- **B–C. Skim attack** (D29): statistical power of the engine's sign-test consistency metric per epoch and over the rolling window, and the dispute economics (bounty on total excess vs stake).
- **D. Collusion** (D24): probability that random assignment puts a ring's accounts in both agent slots or all payout-audit seats, by pool size.
- **E. Payout canaries** (D27): time to catch a client that always answers "plausible".
- **F–G. Optimistic verification** (D28, D31): detection vs dispute propensity, sampled-audit rate and colluding auditors; griefing costs.
- **H. Genesis** (A6, D23): historical credit at the early rate vs the cap; ratified vs never-ratified provisional founder work.
- **I. Audit duty** (D25): supply vs demand of audit runs under optimistic verification.
- **J–L. Governance** (D34, D36–D38): capture thresholds by tier, contributor zero's share of both weights over time, and the per-organization cap.

## Headline results (read with the tables)

1. **The rate ceiling is necessary.** Alone, contributor zero receives 3,000 WOS/week with the ceiling (S9) and 1,986,219 WOS/week without it (S10). Low participation returns about 99% of each budget to the reserve (S6), so nothing is lost; it is emitted later when work exists.
2. **Utilisation-scaled accruals are necessary.** The first run of this simulation found that completion and security accruals, as first drafted, moved 20% of every budget into pools even when almost nobody worked (S6 reached 104M WOS in pools). The engine now accrues pools only in proportion to what the distributing slices emitted; S6 pools stay at about 1.7M WOS after ten years.
3. **Emission is participation-driven, bounded and front-loaded.** With enough participation (S2–S5) about 14% of max supply is issued in year 1, 26% by year 2, 45% by year 4 and 76% by year 10; the per-ACU rate falls both with time and with the number of contributors (about 99 WOS/ACU at 1,000 contributors in week 1, 0.10 at 1,000,000).
4. **Price normalisation moves shares between providers.** A 50% Codex price cut in the oracle drops Codex contributors' execution share from about 40% to about 25% for the same work (S8). A falling oracle for everyone lowers emission once the ceiling binds (S7). Both are why oracle changes are damped (≤ 30% per version) and the ceiling is re-based with oracle versions (REWARD-PROTOCOL section 3).
5. **Usage rewards are gameable up to the cap.** Fabricated but internally consistent run logs at the cap pay about +56% receipt by receipt; only the pattern lookback (an upheld finding opens a review of the last 13 epochs) removes the expected gain, and that result assumes the lookback recovers what it finds (A).
6. **Small skims surface slowly.** A 10% skim on 10 receipts/epoch reaches a 50% chance of being ranked only after ~39 epochs; at 30 receipts/epoch after ~13. Honest accounts are ranked at Z ≥ 3 about 0.1% of the time (B). The total-excess bounty makes a pattern dispute worth filing even when each victim loses cents (C).
7. **Random gates are weak in small pools.** With 10 eligible reviewers a 3-account ring captures both agent slots of its own work 2.8% of the time; with 200 it is 0.01% (D). Below 10 eligible auditors the human sign-off is the gate.
8. **Genesis is small under these rules.** Even 2,000 retro size points value at about 340,000 WOS at the early rate, far below the 5,000,000 WOS cap (H). Provisional founder work that is never ratified contributes nothing live and nothing to Genesis.
9. **Governance: founder mode first.** In a low-participation network (S6) contributor zero holds about 23% of contribution weight and 72% of locked weight, enough to meet routine turnout alone if nobody else votes; with 1,000+ contributors the share is below 2% (K). This is why V1 keeps founder mode until the activation threshold (GOVERNANCE.md section 5).
