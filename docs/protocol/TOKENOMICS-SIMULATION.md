# TOKENOMICS-SIMULATION (DRAFT v4: budget-based rewards D49; pending Astra review 05)

Reproducible simulation of the Proof of Contribution economics and abuse model. Everything below the marker is generated by `tools/tokenomics-sim` from a fixed seed and the protocol's own reward engine (`@waronsaas/contracts/protocol` `computeEpoch`, bigint, conserved funding equation). Nothing in it is data about real people: there are no contributors yet. All inputs are stated assumptions, chosen to explore the design, and the V1 policy values themselves are provisional (D33).

## How to run

```
source ~/.nvm/nvm.sh && nvm use 22            # Node >= 22.18 (runs .ts directly)
npm run sim:tokenomics                         # builds the contracts, prints the tables
node tools/tokenomics-sim/run.ts --write       # refreshes the tables in this file
node tools/tokenomics-sim/run.ts --check       # exits 1 if this file is stale (also run by the test suite)
node tools/tokenomics-sim/preview.ts <new-reward-policy.json>   # what-if: re-runs the scenarios under a candidate policy and diffs them (D33)
```

## What the simulation models (v4: budget-based rewards, D49)

- **Epoch scenarios S1–S10** (520 weekly epochs = 10 years, checkpoints shown). Active contributors are split into 20 lognormal quantile cohorts (mean 20 ACU/week, sigma 0.8, so the median contributor produces about 14.5 ACU/week), 60% on Claude and 40% on Codex, with seeded noise. **Each epoch the work queue is issued as tasks whose budgets (from the budget model) equal the ACU the work is expected to need**; planning tasks add 10% of execution; human-review tasks 1 ACU per merged unit (12 ACU); proposals are outcomes (10 ACU-equivalents per 50 units). The issuance rate is min(ceiling, task capacity / queued demand), fixed per task; tasks issued in epoch e are accepted in e+1 and paid their reservation. Contributor zero (the founder) is modelled at 30 ACU/week under the same rules. S7: the budget model recalibrates to a 40%/year price fall; S8: a 50% Codex price cut, which moves telemetry only; S9/S10 contributor zero alone with and without the ceiling.
- **A. Waste and fabrication** (Astra-01 item 5, D49): what each usage behaviour would have paid under v3 usage-based pay if never caught, and what it pays now (1.00× by construction).
- **A2. Fabricated usage by detection rate** (Astra-02 H4, Astra-03 M13): the v3 Monte Carlo (holdback 50%/13 epochs, compensatory vs punitive recovery, exit, churn, contaminated baselines) beside the D49 result (0%).
- **N. Budget inflation** (D49): a proposer and a colluding builder raise budgets over the model; consensus review, the human-approval threshold, the hard maximum and the peer ranking of budgets over 13 epochs.
- **O. Splitting / stacking** (D49): model budgets of one unit vs k split units, with and without the per-objective cap.
- **P. Cherry-picking and stale budgets** (D49): how long an overpriced task class lasts under recalibration, and the average overpay under a 40%/year price fall.
- **D. Collusion** (D24): probability that random assignment puts a ring's accounts in both agent slots or all payout-audit seats, by pool size.
- **E. Payout canaries** (D27): time to catch a client that always answers "plausible".
- **F–G. Optimistic verification** (D28, D31): detection vs dispute propensity, sampled-audit rate and colluding auditors; griefing costs.
- **H. Genesis** (A6, D23): historical credit at the early rate vs the cap; ratified vs never-ratified provisional founder work.
- **I. Audit duty** (D25): supply vs demand of audit runs under optimistic verification.
- **J–L. Governance** (D34, D36–D38): capture thresholds by tier, contributor zero's share of both weights over time, and the per-organization cap (eligibility applied before caps, Astra-03 H8).
- Removed in v4: B–C (usage skim; now budget inflation, N) and M (quiet-epoch timing; the price is fixed at issuance).

## Headline results (read with the tables)

1. **Fabricated usage gains nothing, by construction (A, A2).** Under v3 a cheater with fabricated but consistent logs at the cap gained about +30% at 0.1% detection per receipt even with a 50% holdback and recovery (+53% with contaminated baselines). Under D49 the same behaviour pays 0% more at any detection rate, and an efficient contributor who uses half the typical tokens keeps the full budget (v3 paid them half).
2. **The new risks are bounded and visible.** Budget inflation by a colluding proposer yields +5% to +26% extra pay per inflated unit over 13 epochs depending on review quality and ring size, capped by the 2× hard maximum and zero-sum inside an acceptance objective (N). Splitting a unit into k units adds +9% to +67% without the per-objective cap and 0% with it (O). A +50% overpriced task class is corrected in about two recalibrations (26 epochs); stale budgets overpay about 6% on average with 13-epoch recalibration, 73% if never recalibrated (P). Unlike usage, budgets are public before work starts.
3. **The ceiling is still necessary.** Alone, contributor zero receives about 3,000 WOS/week with the ceiling (S9) and about 2,000,000 WOS/week without it (S10, where the rate becomes capacity / one person's demand). Low participation leaves about 99% of each budget in the reserve (S6).
4. **Emission is participation-driven, bounded and front-loaded.** With enough participation (S2–S5) about 13% of max supply is issued in year 1, 25% by year 2, 43% by year 4 and 75% by year 10. The issuance rate falls ex ante with participation (about 100 WOS/ACU at 1,000 contributors in week 1, 10 at 10,000, 0.10 at 1,000,000); reservation at issuance with the demand-based rate issues every queued task in the scenarios (0% not issued), so nothing accepted is ever scaled.
5. **Provider prices no longer move shares.** A 50% Codex price cut leaves Codex contributors at about 40% of accepted execution (S8; v3: 25%). A budget model that tracks falling prices lowers emission once the ceiling binds (S7: 96% of the year-10 budget stays in reserve), so the ceiling must be re-based with each model version.
6. **Utilisation-scaled accruals remain necessary**: pools accrue only in proportion to the funded task capacity (S6 pools stay at about 1.7M WOS after ten years).
7. **Random gates are weak in small pools.** With 10 eligible reviewers a 3-account ring captures both agent slots of its own work 2.8% of the time; with 200 it is 0.01% (D). Below 10 eligible auditors the human sign-off is the gate — and with D49 also the budget review.
8. **Genesis is small under these rules.** At the mean issuance rate of epochs 1–12 of a 1,000-contributor network (~98 WOS/ACU), 2,000 retro size points value at about 785,000 WOS, below the 5,000,000 WOS cap (H).
9. **Governance: founder mode first.** In a low-participation network (S6) contributor zero holds about 23% of contribution weight and 68% of locked weight, enough to meet routine turnout alone if nobody else votes; with 1,000+ contributors the share is below 2% (K).
10. **Organization caps hold (H5, Astra-03 H8).** With 50 independent voters an organization with 90% of the raw weight ends at exactly 10% and cannot pass anything alone; with only 5 other voters the tally refuses (L).
